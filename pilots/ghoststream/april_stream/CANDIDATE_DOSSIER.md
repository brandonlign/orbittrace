# GhostStream April stream candidate dossier

## Executive assessment

GhostStream has identified a **high-confidence uncatalogued meteor-stream candidate** active in late April. The candidate was found blindly in 2026 Global Meteor Network data, confirmed in frozen GMN templates across 2022–2025, and supported by matching meteors in two independently processed historical video-orbit archives.

The evidence is strong enough to prepare an IAU Meteor Data Center submission and manuscript. It is not strong enough to claim official recognition or an established shower.

## Candidate identity

Internal label: `GhostStream-April-36.9`

No official IAU designation or shower name has been assigned. Under the current IAU nomenclature procedure, a new submission initially receives a provisional designation rather than a discoverer-chosen official name.

## Discovery chronology

1. The GhostStream method first passed known-shower recovery and weak-stream injection gates.
2. A 2025 all-season search found no defensible uncatalogued annual shower.
3. A fully untouched 2026 January–July scan produced one serious structure, in April.
4. The April template was frozen before historical validation.
5. Exact-time deduplication and year-by-year GMN tests confirmed the structure in 2022–2026.
6. A second audit removed orbital elements from activity selection and embedded the candidate inside a widened antihelion-background null.
7. The frozen GMN solution was applied without refitting to legacy CAMS and permanent SonotaCo catalogs.
8. A NASA/JPL parent-body screen found no credible known parent.

### Antihelion-source audit

The candidate sits near the conventional boundary of the broad antihelion source, so the final null deliberately widened that source to Sun-centered longitude 120°–240°, |β| ≤ 35°, and Vg = 15–50 km/s.

The activity selection used only:

- Sun-centered radiant longitude;
- ecliptic latitude;
- geocentric speed;
- solar longitude.

No orbital element or node entered the activity selection. Orbit was tested afterward against source- and time-matched null groups.

Untouched-year results:

- pooled activity p = 1.857×10⁻⁵;
- empirical shifted-window p = 0.01754;
- pooled source-matched orbit-null p = 0.0001.

This removes the main orbital-node/date circularity objection and shows that the candidate is a narrow enhancement inside, rather than merely outside, a broad antihelion background definition.

## Novelty and duplicate audit

### IAU Meteor Data Center

The audit parsed 2,174 official shower solutions, including working-list solutions. No hard match passed the activity, drifted-radiant, speed, and orbit criteria.

The nearest official orbit was Northern May Ophiuchids at D ≈ 0.235. It is inactive at the candidate epoch and remains separated in radiant and orbit.

### Literature search

Targeted searches used the candidate epoch, equatorial radiant, geocentric speed, and orbital elements. No published shower matching the complete solution was found. Similar-sounding April Ophiuchid showers have materially different radiants, speeds, or orbits.

This search is not proof of novelty. Expert duplicate review remains mandatory because minor-shower literature and coordinate conventions are fragmented.

## Parent-body search

A NASA/JPL SBDB screen tested 6,284 objects in a broad compatible orbit box:

- 0 objects at orbital D ≤ 0.15;
- 16 objects at D ≤ 0.25.

The nearest object, at D ≈ 0.159, had an uncertainty-code-8 orbit based on an 11-day arc. It is not credible parent-body evidence. No parent association is claimed.

## Strongest objections and current answers

### “This is just the antihelion source.”

The candidate survives as a narrow activity enhancement inside an expanded antihelion background. The activity test does not use orbit, and the selected radiant-time core is independently orbitally compact.

### “The node forces the date, making the significance circular.”

The final activity and shifted-window tests use no orbital element. Orbit is tested only after selection.

### “GMN labels or duplicate solutions created the cluster.”

Only trajectories labeled sporadic entered the discovery analysis. Exact-time duplicate solutions were collapsed to the best-quality solution. The stream recurs across many countries, stations, nights, and years.

### “This is a known shower under another solution.”

No IAU solution passes the combined activity/radiant/speed/orbit duplicate veto. The nearest official orbit remains too far away. This is still subject to expert review.

### “The result is network-specific.”

Ten matching meteors appear in CAMS and SonotaCo. Their pooled evidence is significant and orbitally close to GMN, but the pooled test is post-hoc and each catalog is individually sparse. A fresh independent pipeline is still needed.
